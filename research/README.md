## Theses

### Master's Thesis
**Institution:** Lebanese American University

My master's research focused on soil-structure interaction and the design of large shallow foundations, with particular attention to how the modulus of subgrade reaction is represented in engineering analysis. The work examined the limitations of using a single uniform stiffness value beneath mat foundations and explored more realistic spatially varying representations to improve settlement and structural response predictions. This research formed the basis for subsequent conference and journal work on the Discrete Area Method and variable subgrade reaction modeling.

### PhD Dissertation
**Institution:** The University of Texas at Austin

My doctoral research investigated PFAS transport and remediation in contaminated subsurface systems. The work combined laboratory experiments, field-scale environmental data, and numerical modeling to evaluate contaminant migration, treatment performance, and the processes that control PFAS retention and release. A major focus was translating fundamental transport behavior into practical remediation strategies for complex field conditions, including work associated with a U.S. Department of Defense environmental remediation project.

## Journal Publication

### Implementation of the Discrete Area Method and Its Impact on the Steel Reinforcement of Large Mat Foundations

**Authors:** Hassan Alzoaby, Gaby Saad, and Grace Abou-Jaoude  
**Journal:** Innovative Infrastructure Solutions  
**Published:** 08 March 2025  
**Volume / Article:** 10, 131  
**DOI:** 10.1007/s41062-025-01926-x

This study evaluates how the spatial distribution of the modulus of subgrade reaction affects the structural design of large mat foundations. Instead of representing the supporting soil with one constant Winkler spring stiffness, the work implements the iterative Discrete Area Method to capture variable foundation support across the mat. The approach was validated using finite-element analysis and compared with conventional constant-stiffness modeling. The results show that accounting for spatially variable soil stiffness can materially change the distribution of bending demands and steel reinforcement, leading to a more rational and optimized foundation design.

My contribution included development of the engineering analysis, interpretation of soil-structure interaction behavior, evaluation of reinforcement impacts, and preparation of the manuscript.

[View journal article on Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41062-025-01926-x)

## Conference Publication

### The Effect of the Variation of the Modulus of Subgrade Reaction on the Design of Large Shallow Foundations

**Authors:** Grace Abou-Jaoude and Hassan Alzoaby  
**Conference:** 20th International Conference on Soil Mechanics and Geotechnical Engineering  
**Location:** Sydney  
**Theme:** TC103

This work investigates the effect of using spatially variable rather than uniform modulus-of-subgrade-reaction values in large shallow foundation design. A semi-coupled soil-structure interaction workflow was used to iteratively exchange foundation pressures and soil stiffness between geotechnical and structural analyses. Parametric studies demonstrated that preserving the actual spatial variability of foundation support can significantly affect predicted structural response and required steel reinforcement. The work highlights the importance of better geotechnical-structural integration in mat foundation design.

[View publication through ISSMGE](https://www.issmge.org/publications/publication/the-effect-of-the-variation-of-the-modulus-of-subgrade-reaction-on-the-design-of-large-shallow-foundations)
